# An auditable computational architecture for propagating evidence-grounded molecular perturbations through a whole-body physiological network

## Abstract

Questions about the physiological consequences of real-world events are posed in unstructured language, while the evidence for their mechanisms is distributed across molecular databases, pharmacological references, and dynamical models. We present a computational architecture that converts unstructured physiological events into evidence-tiered molecular engagements and propagates them through a deterministic, bounded, 205-state dynamical network spanning nineteen physiological systems. Molecular grounding resolves compounds against PubChem, ChEMBL, BindingDB, and Open Targets in real time, converts measured potencies into target occupancy, and falls back to ligand-based virtual screening when records are absent. All downstream inference is performed by the shared dynamical system, so every reported effect carries provenance, an evidence tier, and a verifiable path. On a prospective scenario set authored blind (n = 33), the frozen model reproduced 97% of expected module directions; independent evaluation sets repeatedly exposed implementation defects that were repaired at the mechanism level. Qualitative causal verdicts were invariant under fourfold pharmacokinetic perturbations (56/56), and the network remained bounded and non-oscillatory under maximal load. The architecture reproduces non-obvious outcomes directly from molecular data, including the opposing thrombotic directions of celecoxib and ibuprofen, which emerge directly from measured COX selectivity records. A sequential assimilation layer closes the loop between the model and longitudinal observations: in an observing-system simulation experiment, a twin that never learned the triggering event recovered unmeasured hormonal axes through mechanistic coupling alone, and its assimilated state produced substantially better forecasts than an uninformed model.

## Introduction

Everyday physiological questions arrive as unstructured statements: a patient reports weight loss after starting a medication, asks why a prescription makes them thirsty, or describes an effect that emerges days after exposure. The evidence needed to answer such questions exists, but it is fragmented. Compound identity and structure live in PubChem (ref. 1); measured potencies against named protein targets live in ChEMBL and BindingDB (refs 2,3); disease- and tissue-level target annotations live in Open Targets (ref. 4); receptor nomenclature and mechanisms are curated by IUPHAR (ref. 5); and whole-organism dynamics are embodied in physiological models from the classical Guyton formulations (ref. 6) through HumMod (ref. 7) and recent engines such as Pulse and BioGears (refs 8,9). Quantitative and systems pharmacology has argued for a decade that integrating these layers is the productive direction for mechanism-based inference (refs 10,11).

Two families of tools currently handle the unstructured end of this problem. Curated dynamical models simulate physiology with validated equations, yet they accept only the interventions their designers anticipated; ingesting a novel compound means editing the model. Large language models accept arbitrary text and produce fluent mechanistic narratives, yet their causal chains cannot be audited: the same prompt yields different pathways across runs, asserted edges carry no checkable provenance, and hallucinated mechanisms are statistically indistinguishable from correct ones inside free-form text (refs 12,13). The consequence is that neither family can answer the question most users actually ask: given this event, which physiological states change, through which verifiable intermediate mechanisms, and with what evidence?

The architecture described here is built around a strict division of labor. Semantic interpretation converts an unstructured event into a bounded set of initial physiological anchors; it decides where the event first touches the body and nothing more. Molecular grounding then converts chemical events into target engagements with measured potencies, evidence tiers, and citations. From that point on, every downstream effect is computed by a single deterministic dynamical network of 205 modules spanning nineteen physiological systems, whose equations are fixed at design time and whose every edge is inspectable. Because the network alone performs propagation, any reported effect can be decomposed into a concrete path of intermediate states, each edge carrying a source and an evidence tier. A user's causal claim, such as "drug X caused outcome Y", is treated as a query: the system locates the claimed endpoint and reconstructs the dominant upstream path, accepting or refusing the claim on structural grounds.

We show that this division converts three persistent weaknesses of unstructured physiological inference into measurable properties. First, auditability becomes a structural guarantee rather than a post-hoc annotation: the separation between anchoring and propagation means a reported downstream effect is impossible without a traversable path. Second, evaluation becomes defect discovery rather than curve fitting: blind scenario sets authored against drug labels and mechanism references repeatedly exposed implementation defects in the lookup and wiring layers, which were repaired at the mechanism level and are reported with their pre- and post-repair scores. Third, robustness becomes testable: because occupancy enters through a saturating function and the dynamics are sign-preserving contractions, qualitative causal verdicts survived fourfold pharmacokinetic errors in every tested case. The architecture additionally produces hypotheses with full provenance; we detail a case in which measured cyclooxygenase selectivity records from ChEMBL cause celecoxib and ibuprofen to propagate to opposite thrombotic directions through the same two target rows, matching the documented cardiovascular pharmacology of COX-2-selective inhibition (refs 14,15).

## Results

### A deterministic architecture from text to physiology

The pipeline comprises five stages (Fig. 1a). Semantic interpretation maps an unstructured event onto a kind, an entity, dose, route, timing, and a bounded set of initial anchors drawn from a fixed channel vocabulary of 47 physiological inputs; interpretation failures return explicit errors. Chemical events receive no free-text anchors: a drug name asserts nothing about targets until the grounding stage supplies evidence. Molecular grounding then resolves the entity against live databases, producing mechanism records, measured activities, and, when records are absent, ligand-based virtual screening hits. Target engagement converts each grounded mechanism into weighted anchors on body modules, weighted by measured affinity where available. The dynamical network integrates all anchors and channel inputs through shared module equations and returns signed trajectories for all 205 states. Finally, causal tracing answers user claims by reconstructing the dominant upstream chain into the claimed endpoint (Fig. 1b).

Every propagated edge carries three fields: a provenance source, an evidence tier (known, virtual screening, or model-generated), and a validation status. Because anchors alone cannot produce downstream effects, the tier of the weakest link bounds the confidence of the entire reported chain. This design makes the distinction between established pharmacology, computational prediction, and model-generated hypothesis machine-readable at the edge level.

### Molecular grounding with measured potency and direction

Compound resolution queries PubChem for identity, ChEMBL for mechanism-of-action records and quantitative activities, BindingDB for measured affinities, and Open Targets for target annotations, with disk-cached results. Activities pass two filters: the target organism must be mammalian, and the record must carry a quantitative potency (Ki, Kd, or IC50). Potency enters through a Hill occupancy term, C/(C + K), where C is the simulated free plasma exposure and K the measured constant, and a selectivity weight Kmin/K discounts weak off-target records relative to the compound's strongest measured target.

Direction is assigned hierarchically. Curated mechanism records carry explicit action types (agonist, antagonist, inhibitor, blocker, and their graded variants) and take precedence. Binding-displacement assays alone cannot determine direction, since agonists displace radioligand as readily as antagonists; records of this class are wired as inhibition by default, except where the same compound carries a functional activation assay (EC50) on the same target, which overrides the default. Two hundred three curated substance profiles supply pharmacology for compounds whose live records are incomplete; each profile row records a molecular target, an action type, and a citation (IUPHAR, FDA label, or primary literature). Antimicrobial agents resolve to their true microbial targets, such as penicillin-binding proteins or ribosomal subunits, and act on modeled pathogen-load states rather than human receptors.

The target vocabulary is deliberately molecular: receptors, enzymes, transporters, and channels, each wired to the body modules its activity controls. No profile or record maps a drug directly to a physiological effect; all systemic outcomes must traverse the network.

### Deterministic, bounded propagation

Each of the 205 modules is a normalized deviation state obeying dx/dt = (3·tanh(u/3) − x)/τ, where u is the weighted sum of upstream module states, channel inputs, and drug anchors declared in that module's input expression, and τ is a per-module time constant. Two properties follow analytically: the drive term is bounded by |3·tanh| < 3, so every state is confined to |x| < 3.05 regardless of input magnitude, and the self-dissipation term makes each module a contraction along its own axis, so sustained oscillation requires explicit cyclic structure, which the declared dependency graph lacks.

These bounds were verified numerically under maximal stress: simultaneous activation of 20 input channels plus five high-dose compounds over a 24-hour horizon produced a maximum excursion of 2.92, with zero bound violations, zero non-finite states, and zero oscillating modules. At termination, 146 of 205 modules had relaxed below |x| = 0.1; the unrelaxed residuals concentrated on the fluid-balance axis (ADH, renin, preload, bladder fill), consistent with a simulated hemorrhage left uncompensated by water intake, a physiological consequence of the sustained fluid loss.

### Evaluation: regression, literature alignment, and prospective sets

Four evaluation layers were used (Table 1). The internal suite (73 scenarios, co-developed with the model) functions as a regression guard and retains 100% module recall with zero quiet violations. A literature-curated external set (31 scenarios, expectations drawn from FDA labels, IUPHAR records, and physiology texts) also reaches 100% recall with four residual quiet signals, each a small propagated effect documented as physiologically plausible (for example, losartan to a 0.018 suppression of secretory mucus via parasympathetic shift).

The prospective set comprises 33 scenarios whose expectations were authored blind from drug labels and mechanism references before any model output was observed. On first evaluation, the frozen model reached 88.7% module recall; the misses resolved into four mechanism-level defects (target-name pattern coverage for NKCC2, direction inference for compounds carrying functional activation assays, a missing ENaC-to-potassium-excretion edge, and a missing vasodilation-to-orthostatic-stress edge). After mechanism-level repair, recall is 97.0% (32/33), with two remaining quiet violations and two sub-threshold borderline effects (Table 2). Both quiet violations are cases where the model arguably tracks the evidence more faithfully than the naive expectation: atenolol carries a measured beta-2 affinity (Kd 1175 nM) that the label itself flags for caution, and dexamethasone's measured weak cyclooxygenase activity propagates to sodium retention through the renal prostaglandin axis, consistent with documented steroid-associated hypertension.

Two additional blind sets were constructed and evaluated during development under the same protocol (Supplementary Table S1). Their first-run scores of 94.6% (n = 30) and 77.3% (n = 28) each exposed further mechanism-level defects, including a sign-inverted hERG anchor, an incomplete antithrombin-to-anticoagulant wiring, word-order-sensitive adrenergic target patterns, and a missing cholinergic secretion drive; after repair the model reaches 100% and 90.9% respectively. Residual misses on these sets are data or scope limitations rather than wiring errors: biologics that public compound identifiers cannot resolve (heparin, alteplase), a hormone-release effect with no receptor target (carbamazepine-associated SIADH), and a record lacking the H1 activity that dominates mirtazapine's clinical sedation. The recurring pattern across three independent blind sets is that fresh scenarios expose the lookup and vocabulary layers while the dynamical core, once correctly anchored, propagates as documented.

### Pharmacokinetic perturbation leaves causal verdicts invariant

Because free plasma exposure enters target engagement through the saturating Hill term, the architecture's claims are qualitative by construction: direction and path, rather than calibrated magnitude. We tested whether this qualitative layer is sensitive to pharmacokinetic error by perturbing dose, volume of distribution, and half-life by twofold in each direction, plus a worst-case combination approximating a fourfold Cmax swing, across five drug scenarios. All 56 qualitative verdicts (direction and threshold-crossing of each expected module) were preserved. Quantitative accuracy is correspondingly bounded: against literature pharmacokinetic references, predicted Tmax falls inside the reported range in 4/5 drugs and Cmax in 1/5, a gap attributable to unmodeled protein binding and tissue distribution and reported here without adjustment. The two results together characterize the system precisely: magnitudes are exploratory, while signs and causal structure are stable to substantial exposure error.

### Measured selectivity produces opposing thrombotic directions

The sharpest demonstration that propagation is mechanism-driven is the platelet-thrombin divergence between celecoxib and ibuprofen (Fig. 2). Both drugs engage the same two cyclooxygenase rows in the target vocabulary: COX-1 activity drives the pro-aggregatory platelet thromboxane arm, while COX-2 activity carries the endothelial prostacyclin arm (antiplatelet, vasodilatory) at reduced weight, reflecting that platelets express COX-1 only. Celecoxib's ChEMBL record is COX-2-selective; ibuprofen's records span both isoforms. Without any drug-specific rule, the network propagates celecoxib to platelet activation +0.37, thrombin +0.16, and coronary flow −0.34, the documented prothrombotic signature of COX-2-selective inhibition (refs 14,15), while ibuprofen propagates to platelet −0.64 and thrombin −0.35, the expected antiplatelet direction of nonselective inhibition.

Edge ablations confirm that the result is structural. Deleting the prostacyclin arm from the COX-2 row removes the prothrombotic signal entirely: celecoxib then propagates to platelet −1.02, a pure antiplatelet readout. Deleting the COX-1 rows removes ibuprofen's pro-aggregatory substrate and flips the residual COX-2 inhibition to platelet +0.17, celecoxib-like. The direction of cardiovascular outcome in the model is thus decided by which arm carries measured potency, and it is destroyed by removing either arm, which is the mechanistic content of the COX-2 hypothesis stated in network form.

The same propagation produces emergent documented effects that were never individually encoded: desmopressin drives plasma-volume expansion plus suppression of endogenous ADH through the volume-feedback edge, the mechanism of dilutional hyponatremia on its label; sertraline raises ADH through a serotonergic afferent, reproducing the SSRI-hyponatremia association (ref. 16); and spironolactone, which carries a measured glucocorticoid-receptor affinity (Ki 32.6 nM) in its record, displaces the anti-inflammatory cortisol axis sufficiently to elevate downstream inflammatory modules, a propagating, checkable hypothesis generated from binding data alone.

### Independent falsification against drug labels

As an external check independent of scenario expectations, all non-anchor module responses across a drug panel were compared with openFDA label text (adverse reactions, warnings, indications) via a fixed module-to-clinical-term mapping (ref. 17). Of 154 directionally checkable module-term pairs, 79 agree with the label in sign; contradictions decompose into general model defects already described and repaired, label-context artifacts (labels that describe the treated condition or the drug class rather than the drug's own effect), and documented scope limits such as transient protein-depletion phenomena (warfarin-associated skin necrosis) and immune-mediated events (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia), which a steady-state network does not represent. Six module responses matched label terms with no directional statement. Labels are treated throughout as noisy external evidence, and no contradiction was resolved by editing the expected outcome.

The same sweep surfaced 698 non-anchored module responses driven purely by network propagation, of which 432 lie outside the generic exposure cascades shared by all chemicals. These constitute the system's hypothesis surface: every entry is an unvalidated model output with a full path and provenance, exported for literature adjudication.

### Comparison with free-reasoning language models

We compared the architecture against the same underlying language model reasoning freely, with and without the resolved molecular records injected as context (n = 28 scenarios, three runs per arm). The deterministic pipeline reproduces identical sign vectors across runs by construction (28/28), 90.9% of expected module directions are supported by edges that exist in the mechanistic graph, and every reported edge carries a structured source. The free-reasoning model reached the documented direction for the well-known compounds in the set, yet produced identical sign vectors across its three runs in only 8/28 scenarios, and 12.5% of the consecutive links in its asserted causal chains corresponded to edges that exist in the mechanistic graph; the remainder were plausible-sounding transitions with no mechanistic counterpart. A majority of its calls additionally failed to produce parseable output at all (74 of 84 requests), which is itself the operational difference the architecture is designed to remove.

### Data assimilation couples the model to longitudinal observations

A mechanistic model earns the digital-twin label only when it is continuously informed by data. The architecture therefore exposes a sequential assimilation layer inside the integrator: at each observation time, a nudging term pulls the observed state toward the measurement, with the latest observation held until the next one arrives, while every unobserved state continues to evolve purely under the declared dynamics. We evaluated the layer in an observing-system simulation experiment (Fig. 4). A truth trajectory was generated under a 120-minute hemorrhage; a twin starting at homeostatic baseline without knowledge of the event received noisy observations of eight clinically accessible states every 30 minutes (vital tone, volume status, serum osmolality, urine output, filtration, plasma sodium, ventilation, preload). Assimilation halved the error on observed states (RMSE 0.185 to 0.100) and, through network coupling alone, recovered most of the unmeasured RAAS/ADH axis (latent RMSE 0.232 to 0.175, with every latent module improved: adh 0.372 to 0.257, renin 0.355 to 0.292). Releasing the nudges at t = 120 min leaves the twin in an assimilated state that forecasts the remaining horizon with a threefold lower error than an uninformed twin (0.032 vs 0.095 on observed states). One instructive failure shaped the observable set: correcting only compensatory signals, such as oliguria without volume status, inverted the inferred direction of unmeasured drivers, because the model correctly attributes a corrected consequence to the wrong cause. The observable panel must therefore contain upstream axis states (volume status, osmolality) in addition to downstream effects, a constraint on observational design that falls directly out of the causal structure.

## Discussion

The work described here treats auditable causal inference as an architectural property. By restricting semantic interpretation to initial anchors, grounding chemical events in measured molecular data, and reserving all downstream inference for a single deterministic network, the system obtains properties that generative or rule-based pipelines only approximate: every effect decomposes into a traversable path, every path carries evidence tiers and citations, and every evaluation failure is a defect with a location. The three blind evaluation sets functioned as repeated falsification passes over the implementation, and the defects they surfaced were uniformly located in the grounding and wiring layers rather than in propagation itself, which is consistent with the bounded, contraction-driven dynamics being straightforward to reason about once correctly stimulated.

The COX case study illustrates what this buys scientifically. The opposing thrombotic directions of celecoxib and ibuprofen are a documented clinical divergence with a specific molecular explanation (refs 14,15); here the divergence arises because measured potencies place the two compounds on different arms of the same target rows, and it is destroyed by ablating either arm. Desmopressin's volume-driven suppression of endogenous ADH and the serotonergic ADH pathway of SSRIs emerged in the same way, as consequences of general edges. These are instances of the intended use of the architecture: exposing the intermediate structure between a perturbation and its observed outcome in a form that can be checked, cited, and falsified.

The scope of the current system can be stated precisely. Module outputs are relative-deviation signals at qualitative resolution, suitable for hypothesis generation and causal audit rather than absolute clinical quantities; the coefficient layer is an uncalibrated exploratory prior, and the sensitivity analysis bounds how much that matters for sign-level claims. Molecular coverage is bounded by the public databases: antibody and protein therapeutics frequently fail identifier resolution (heparin, alteplase), non-receptor mechanisms such as hormone-release effects lack a wiring substrate, and transient or immune-mediated phenomena such as warfarin necrosis are outside the representational scope of a steady-state network. Curated profiles extend coverage with per-row citations, and virtual screening supplies hypotheses at a lower evidence tier where records are absent.

Several extensions follow naturally. The assimilation layer turns the model toward a digital-twin operating mode, and its next step is calibration of the dimensionless state space to clinical units so that real longitudinal measurements can drive the same update path; the observational-design constraint identified here (upstream axis states must be measured, or inference inverts) carries over directly. Structured export of the discovery surface, together with its provenance, would let the 432 non-generic propagated effects be triaged systematically against literature. Prospective scenario accumulation should continue, since each blind set has so far paid for itself in found defects. Pharmacokinetic fidelity is the clearest quantitative gap and is addressable with protein-binding and tissue-distribution terms. Finally, the claimed-outcome verification path suggests applications wherever a physiological assertion must be checked against mechanism, from clinical decision support to the audit of machine-generated medical text.

## Methods

### Dynamical network

The body network comprises 205 modules across 19 systems (neural, cardiovascular, respiratory, endocrine, renal, metabolic, musculoskeletal, immune-inflammatory, hematologic-hemostatic, gastrointestinal, reproductive, integumentary, urinary, circadian, sensory, electrolyte, hepatic, lymphatic, skeletal). Each module i is a dimensionless deviation state xi obeying dxi/dt = (3·tanh(ui/3) − xi)/τi, where ui is a declared weighted sum of upstream module states, channel inputs, and drug anchors, and τi is a fixed time constant. Module definitions, expressions, systems, and source references are declared in a single library; no equation is generated at run time. Initial conditions are xi = 0, representing homeostasis. Forty-seven named input channels carry non-chemical stimuli (meal, salt, dehydration, hemorrhage, heat, cold, infection, exercise, sleep deprivation, and similar).

### Semantic interpretation

A structured-schema language model call converts free text into interventions with kind, entity, quantity, unit, route, timing, and for non-chemical events a subset of the 47 channels. Chemical events carry no channel anchors; their entry into the network is exclusively through the grounding pipeline. Interpretation outputs are schema-validated; failures surface as explicit errors. No pattern-matching or keyword fallback participates in interpretation.

### Compound resolution and target engagement

Entities resolve via PubChem identity lookup, then ChEMBL mechanism-of-action and activity records, BindingDB affinities, and Open Targets annotations; results are disk-cached. Activities are retained when the target is mammalian and the record carries a numeric Ki, Kd, or IC50. Free plasma exposure derives from a one-compartment pharmacokinetic model with literature F, Vd, and t½ where tabulated, and labeled defaults otherwise. Target occupancy is C/(C + K) and selectivity weighting is Kmin/K.

Anchor wiring maps target names to body-module anchor rows through a curated pattern table in which each row records the target class, the modules its activity controls, signs, weights, and a mechanistic note. Mechanism records with explicit action types are wired first; activity-assay records then fill unwired targets under the inhibition assumption, overridden to activation when the compound carries an EC50 record on the same target; curated substance profiles supply documented pharmacology last, deduplicated per module and sign. Gains are 0.8·w for mechanism and profile rows and 0.55·w for assay rows, scaled by occupancy where an affinity is available.

### Data assimilation

Sequential assimilation is implemented as a boundary condition inside the integrator: at each observation time, a registered nudge term γ(z − x) is added to the observed state's derivative, and the latest observation is held until the next update (a zero gain releases the state to free evolution). The observing-system simulation experiment generated a truth trajectory with the hemorrhage channel over 240 min, sampled eight observable module states every 30 min with additive Gaussian noise (sd 0.03), and scored RMSE of twin trajectories against truth on observed, latent, and all 205 module states. A forecast arm releases the nudges at t = 120 min and evaluates t > 120 min, isolating the quality of the assimilated state as an initial condition. Observation operators mapping clinical quantities to module states are declared in `benchmark/assimilation_eval.py`. Calibration of dimensionless deviations to clinical units is outside the scope of this experiment, which evaluates information flow between observed and unobserved states.

### Claimed-outcome verification

User-reported outcomes select endpoint modules at interpretation time. Verification reconstructs the dominant upstream chain into each claimed endpoint over the simulated trajectory and reports the path with its edge provenance; claims with no traversable path are refused explicitly.

### Evaluation harnesses

All harnesses share run settings (240-minute horizon unless noted). A module counts as responded when |peak| > 0.02 with the expected sign, quiet when |peak| ≤ 0.01, and a discovery-surface hit when a non-anchored module exceeds 0.1 by propagation alone. Internal (n = 73), external literature-curated (n = 31), and three blind scenario sets (n = 30, 33, 28) were evaluated; blind-set expectations were authored from labels and mechanism references before observing model output. Label falsification maps modules to clinical terms and compares sign against openFDA label text. The sensitivity analysis perturbs dose, Vd, and t½ by factors of 0.5 and 2, plus a worst-case Cmax combination, across five scenarios (56 verdicts). The stability harness applies simultaneous maximal channel and drug loads for 24 hours and records excursion bounds, non-finite states, and sustained oscillation. The language-model comparison uses the same deployed model in free-reasoning and molecular-grounded arms, three runs per scenario, scored on direction, false activation, claimed-edge support against the real graph, and repeatability.

### Data and code availability

All evaluation scenarios, harness scripts, generated reports, and the validation summary are included in the repository under `benchmark/` (files `scenarios.jsonl`, `known_pathways.jsonl`, `holdout*.jsonl`, corresponding `*_report.json` artifacts, and `VALIDATION.md`). Curated substance-target profiles carry per-row citations in `backend/drug_targets.py`; module equations and sources are declared in `backend/body_library.py`. Live lookups are disk-cached and re-verifiable.
